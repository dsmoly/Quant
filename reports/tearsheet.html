Dealer market making: did it make money, and where is the edge?

Monte Carlo results for five market-making configurations built from Cartea–Donnelly–Jaimungal robust quoting and the Assayag–Barzykin–Cont–Xiong mean field game, run on a simulator that is misspecified in both papers' senses at once.

120 paths per arm 20-minute episodes common random numbers inventory limit ±8 Nash benchmark 0.02049

The short answer

Yes — every configuration makes money under the papers' own calibration, and the profitable ones do it by capturing quoted spread, not by predicting price. Under toxic flow the arms separate sharply, and one of them loses money outright. There is no directional alpha anywhere in this strategy: inventory P&L is negative in all ten arm/regime combinations. Every unit of profit comes from being paid to provide immediacy, and the entire skill is in how much of that payment survives adverse selection.

Read the Sharpe numbers as a ranking, not a forecast

A per-episode Sharpe near 10 with a 100% win rate is not a realistic trading result — it is what a frictionless simulator produces. There are no fees, no latency, no minimum tick, no funding cost on inventory, no gap risk, and the market maker observes the true midprice exactly. These numbers are valid for comparing arms on identical paths, which is what they are used for here. They are not a claim about live performance.

Where the edge lies

Terminal wealth decomposes exactly. Each fill changes inventory by dq and cash by −dq·S + δ, so summing over fills and applying Abel summation gives

P&L = Σδ + Σ dq(S_T − S_i)

— gross spread capture plus position P&L, and the position term splits at a 5-second markout horizon into adverse selection and residual inventory P&L. This is an identity, not a regression: the four components sum to the reported P&L to floating point on every path, which the code asserts.

What this says

  • The edge is liquidity provision, not prediction. Gross spread capture exceeds net P&L in every single arm, in both regimes. Everything else on the chart is a cost. There is no term in which the strategy profits from the direction of the midprice.
  • Inventory P&L is negative everywhere — from −0.07 to −2.65 across the ten combinations. Holding a position is a cost the strategy pays to make markets, never a source of return. Any story about this strategy "trading a view" would be false.
  • The capture ratio is the whole game. It falls from ~95% under benign flow to ~42% under toxic flow. The market maker's job is not to earn a wider spread — the gross figures barely move between arms — but to keep more of the spread it already quotes.
  • The one losing arm has a capture ratio of −0.2%. Under toxic flow, neutral / adaptive-ref gives back 100.2% of everything it earns. Adverse selection consumes the entire gross spread and then some, which is precisely the failure the robust framework exists to prevent.

The daily-equivalent Sharpe scales the per-episode figure by the square root of how many episodes fit in a 6.5-hour day, assuming episodes are independent — they are, by construction here, but that assumption is exactly what fails in a real market, so treat that row as arithmetic rather than evidence. What these numbers are not

A simulator that shares the strategy's assumptions cannot validate it

It can refute — and it did, killing the original regime-switching hypothesis on out-of-sample grounds. But the effects that survived were measured on dynamics taken from the same papers the strategy is built on. Live fill data is the only real test.

  • No frictions at all. No fees, rebates, latency, minimum tick, quote throttling, or funding on inventory. Adding any of them cuts the tighter-quoting arms hardest, since they trade three times as much.
  • Cover feedback is unbiased here. The population quote is estimated from cover prices on lost trades. On a live desk that sample is selection-biased toward tighter competitors — the trades you lose are the ones where someone was aggressive.
  • Competitors are one scalar. Real RFQ flow is client-segmented and not exchangeable; the mean-field abstraction is a strong one.
  • Toxicity is a dial, not a measurement. ε = 0.015 was chosen to show where the arms separate. The true level for any real venue is an empirical question, and it is the single input that most changes which configuration you should run.
  • Two ambiguity budgets are tied. φλ and φκ move together, because the paper only gives a closed form under Proposition 5's symmetry conditions.
